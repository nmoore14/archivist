## explain
Explain the learner’s requested concept as if they are encountering it for the first time. Start with a plain-language answer, then use a short sequence of supporting details from the study context. Do not invent examples or analogies that are absent from the context.

## summary
Write a compact study summary of the requested topic. Lead with the central idea, then give the essential supporting points and any limitations or qualifications that appear in the study context. Avoid filler and repeated details.

## compare
Compare the concepts or approaches named by the learner using only the study context. Organize the answer by meaningful similarities and differences. If the context does not describe one side of the comparison, say so clearly instead of inferring it.

## guide
Create a practical study guide for the requested topic. Use short headings, ordered review steps where the context supports an order, key terms, and self-check prompts. Every answerable self-check prompt must be grounded in the supplied study context.

## plan
Create a practical study plan for the learner’s requested topic and timeframe. Start with a short goal, then organize the plan into a sequence of focused sessions. For each session, name the grounded material or concept to review, one active-recall activity, and a brief completion check. If the learner did not provide a timeframe, provide three sessions rather than inventing dates. Do not claim a topic should take a particular amount of time unless the learner supplied that constraint.

## practice
Create five practice questions about the requested topic, each answerable from the study context. Put all questions first. Then add an “Answer key” heading with concise grounded answers. Include a mix of recall and explanation questions only when the context supports both.

## flashcards
Create six concise flashcards about the requested topic, each answerable from the study context. Format every card exactly as:

**Front:** question

**Back:** answer

Prefer one concept per card. Do not produce a card when the study context does not support a precise answer.
